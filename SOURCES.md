# Sources — what each panel of entry.png is based on

All 30 stories happened, or were first reported, on Tuesday 17 November 2026.
Compiled 18 November 2026 (WIB) from that day's reporting; claims that rest on a
single interested party are worded as claims in the image. Numbering matches the
map dots (1 = lead, 2–13 = the twelve cards, 14–30 = the briefs).

## The lead and the twelve cards

- **1 — Lead, Crimea Bridge.** Ukrainian strike collapsed a road span of the
  Kerch Bridge; Zelenskyy called it an "unprecedented operation"; Russian
  missiles hit Kyiv's Dnipro bridges in reply. (BBC reporting via Level
  Headlines' Daily Brief; Just Security Early Edition; Zark News Daily Briefing;
  Open Chronicle.)
- **2 — Hormuz tankers.** Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two oil tankers struck
  naval mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz; the claim was not
  independently verified. A third US carrier (USS Theodore Roosevelt) joined the
  region; Brent crude ~$92.76. (Reuters and Al Jazeera via Level Headlines and
  Zark News; Bermuda Broadcasting/BBC wire headlines.)
- **3 — Bahrain drones.** Iran said it fired drones at a US radar system at
  Sheikh Isa base; Bahrain said it intercepted the attack, with no casualties or
  damage reported; Washington called the exchange a sharp escalation. (Just
  Security Early Edition.)
- **4 — Trump in Manila.** Arrival in the Philippines for the ASEAN summit; joint
  trade statement signed with Thailand; three-day Asia trip ending in China with
  three-way Xi–Putin talks planned. (Associated Press via Level Headlines; Open
  Chronicle.)
- **5 — Nvidia earnings.** Reported after Tuesday's close: revenue $115.8bn
  (+103% year on year, vs ~$109bn expected), data-centre revenue $107.6bn
  (+110%), adjusted EPS $2.68 vs $2.47, guidance $130bn ±3%. First report since
  co-founder Jensen Huang resigned on 12 November; CFO Colette Kress is interim
  CEO. (Forbes; Gizmodo; BeInCrypto; TechDefused; Investopedia's market preview.)
- **6 — Vulcan failure.** ULA's Vulcan launched Sunday 15 November from Cape
  Canaveral and suffered an anomaly ~10 minutes in; all 40 Amazon Leo satellites
  were lost — ULA's first total mission failure since 2006. On Tuesday ULA said
  early data suggests the first stage underperformed just after liftoff; the FAA
  grounded Vulcan. (Spaceflight Now; GeekWire; Florida Today.)
- **7 — AI-chip loophole.** The Commerce Department moved to curb China's remote
  (cloud) access to American AI computing power / advanced chips. (Zark News;
  Investopedia; Bermuda Broadcasting/BBC wire headlines.)
- **8 — UK sanctions & Gaza aid.** Britain sanctioned eight Israeli construction
  companies involved in West Bank settlements; the UK, Australia, Canada and
  Italy jointly urged Israel to allow rapid humanitarian aid access through
  Gaza's crossings. (Just Security Early Edition; ASAS Press World News Digest;
  Bermuda Broadcasting/BBC wire headlines.)
- **9 — JPMorgan outage.** The bank blamed a software bug — not a cyberattack —
  for Monday's disruption to online banking, branch transactions and Zelle
  payments. (ABC News via Level Headlines.)
- **10 — Measles-free status lost.** The Pan American Health Organization said
  endemic transmission is back in the US, Mexico and Bolivia after the same
  strain circulated for a year or more; the US held the status for 26 years;
  5,400+ US cases in 2026, the most since 1991. (Reuters; PAHO; The Guardian.)
- **11 — EU Rwanda deportation hub.** Five EU states plan to send rejected
  asylum seekers to Rwanda from 2027, Euronews reported; rights groups
  condemned the scheme. (Euronews; The Profiler.)
- **12 — South Africa shootings.** Seventeen people killed in two mass shootings
  in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng on Monday. (Dumpling; The Profiler.)
- **13 — DR Congo Ebola.** Africa CDC said the six-month-old outbreak is still
  not under control and spreading at a record pace: 5,568 dead, 11,376 cases;
  WHO briefing on 17 November said two experimental vaccines enter phase 2
  trials; only $1.3bn of $2.9bn pledged has been released. (Reuters; Al Jazeera;
  Qazinform; ECDC.)

## The seventeen briefs

- **14 — Mongolia's president quits.** President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa resigned over
  corruption allegations; parliament must approve the resignation. (Reuters /
  Montsame; BusinessWorld.)
- **15 — Serbia's student MPs.** The Student List took 140 of 250 parliament
  seats, ending 14 years of SNS rule. (Reuters; France 24; DW.)
- **16 — France strikes.** Nationwide public-sector strike over pay; up to
  300,000 marchers expected. (Al Jazeera; France 24; AFP.)
- **17 — Estonia closes its Russian border.** All road crossings to shut from
  1 December after a GRU-attributed arson attempt on the Threod Systems drone
  factory; eight detained. (Silicon; London Daily; Bytes EU.)
- **18 — Guinea gold-mining crackdown.** 224 people jailed; 1,263 arrested and 10
  killed in clashes; the crackdown drove tens of thousands into Mali. (DW; AP.)
- **19 — Guatemala state of siege.** Congress weighs a 30-day state of siege in
  Zacapa and Izabal as the US designates three drug clans (Los Lorenzanas, Los
  Huistas, Los Mendoza) as foreign terrorist organisations. (AP; US State
  Department; BBC Mundo.)
- **20 — West Bank settler killing.** Settlers killed a 72-year-old Palestinian
  woman near Jericho, officials said. (BBC; Reuters via Just Security.)
- **21 — "Dirty bomb" intelligence.** US intelligence reports say Iran weighed a
  "dirty bomb" attack in Washington, officials told CNN; no attack is confirmed
  and the reports remain unconfirmed publicly. (CNN via Just Security; Open
  Chronicle.)
- **22 — COP31 adaptation target.** The draft cover decision puts adaptation
  finance at at least $120bn a year by 2035, a core demand of African countries.
  (The Government and Business Journal; BP Consulting; Asia Today; Valcored
  News.)
- **23 — Pentagon audit.** The Pentagon failed its financial audit for the ninth
  straight year. (Zark News.)
- **24 — Japan scrambles jets.** Japan scrambled fighter jets and protested a
  suspected Russian airspace violation; Seoul and Taipei responded too. (Just
  Security; ASAS Press.)
- **25 — Pope Leo XIV returns to Rome.** The Pope flew home to Rome on Tuesday
  17 November, landing at Fiumicino at 8:48 a.m. and closing his 12-day
  apostolic journey to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru — his first visit to South
  America as Pope, ending with a farewell Mass for about 1.5 million people in
  Lima. (Holy See Press Office bulletin, 17 November 2026; NPR, 17 November;
  EWTN Vatican, 17 November.)
- **26 — Florida floods.** Flood watches/warnings along Florida's east coast.
  (Daytona Beach News-Journal / National Weather Service.)
- **27 — Andhra cyclone.** A severe cyclone of the north-east monsoon season
  bearing down on India's Andhra Pradesh coast. (The Hindu BusinessLine morning
  report.)
- **28 — Geneva autonomous-weapons talks.** 128 states opened the CCW Review
  Conference in Geneva (16–20 November) on banning lethal autonomous weapons;
  the US, Russia and Israel oppose a binding treaty. (Just Security; UN.org;
  SOFX; Superpower Daily.)
- **29 — APEC in China.** World leaders gathered in China for the APEC Economic
  Leaders' Meeting. (Nepal Times; Open Chronicle.)
- **30 — Australia–Japan research pact.** The Group of Eight and Japan's RU11
  universities deepened a strategic research partnership ahead of Horizon Europe,
  at a high-level dialogue in Australia on 17 November. (Group of Eight media
  release, 17 November; Science|Business.)

## Dropped during research

One candidate brief was dropped for failing the competition's date rule: a South
Sudan certified-accountants institute was announced on 12 November and launches
on 18 November — neither on 17 November. It was replaced by the APEC brief (29).

A second swap fixed the six-continent claim: the EU–Temu brief (25) was replaced
by the Pope's return to Rome, because no brief named a South American story and
the "6 continents" claim needs one. The Pope story was reported on 17 November
by three independent sources (see 25).
