WORLD NEWS, SUMMED UP
EUROPE · WAR

Ukraine blows a gap in the Crimea Bridge

A Ukrainian strike collapsed a road span of the Kerch Bridge, the only link between Russia and occupied Crimea. President Zelenskyy hailed an “unprecedented operation” weeks in the making — and Russian missiles answered the same day against Kyiv’s bridges over the Dnipro.

MIDDLE EAST · ENERGY

Iran says two tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz

The Revolutionary Guards said two oil tankers struck naval mines and caught fire on a route Tehran calls unauthorised — a claim not independently verified. A third US aircraft carrier reached the region, and Brent crude held near $92.76.

ASIA · DIPLOMACY

Trump lands in Manila for the ASEAN summit

The president arrived in the Philippines and signed a joint trade statement with Thailand, opening a three-day Asia trip that ends in China with three-way talks alongside Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin.

MARKETS · TECH

Nvidia beats forecasts in first report since Huang’s exit

Revenue jumped 103% to $115.8bn as data-centre sales hit $107.6bn, and Nvidia guided to $130bn next quarter. It was the chipmaker’s first report since co-founder Jensen Huang resigned; interim chief Colette Kress led the call.

SPACE

Vulcan rocket fails — 40 Amazon satellites lost

ULA said its Vulcan’s first stage underperformed just after Sunday’s liftoff from Cape Canaveral, dooming 40 Amazon Leo internet satellites. It is ULA’s first total mission failure since 2006, and the fleet is grounded.

TECH · TRADE WAR

US moves to close the AI-chip loophole for China

The Commerce Department moved to curb China’s remote access to American AI computing power, tightening the export rules around renting US chips through the cloud — and chip shares climbed on the day.

BRITAIN · GAZA

UK sanctions settlement firms as allies press on Gaza aid

Britain sanctioned eight Israeli construction companies building settlements in the occupied West Bank, while the UK, Australia, Canada and Italy jointly urged Israel to open Gaza’s crossings to humanitarian aid.

UNITED STATES · BANKING

JPMorgan blames a software bug for Monday’s banking outage

The glitch slowed online banking, branch transactions and Zelle payments across the United States. The bank said there was no indication the disruption was a cyberattack.
